Overview

Reimagining personal banking on mobile

Zenith is a digital-first bank targeting millennials and Gen Z users who expect seamless mobile experiences. The existing app, launched 18 months prior, had functional capabilities but suffered from poor usability — a cluttered dashboard, confusing navigation, and a transaction flow that required too many taps. The app had a 3.2-star rating with recurring complaints about "hard to find things" and "too many steps to send money."

I led the complete UI redesign of the iOS and Android apps, working closely with the product team and engineering to deliver a fresh, intuitive experience that would compete with neobank leaders like Revolut and Monzo.

Background

What users were struggling with

A deep-dive into app analytics and support ticket analysis revealed three core problem areas:

  • Dashboard overload: The home screen tried to show everything — account balance, recent transactions, quick actions, promotions, savings goals, and notifications — all competing for attention in a single scrollable view. Users reported feeling "overwhelmed" when opening the app.
  • Transfer friction: Sending money — the #1 action by frequency — required 6 screens: Select recipient → Enter amount → Choose account → Add note → Review → Confirm with biometric. Competitor apps averaged 3 screens.
  • Navigation confusion: The app used a hamburger menu for primary navigation, hiding 12 items behind a single icon. User testing showed 60% of participants couldn't find the "Statements" feature within 30 seconds.
Discovery

Research & competitive benchmarking

User Interviews (10 participants)

I worked with our UX researcher to interview 10 active users (5 power users who logged in daily, 5 casual users who logged in 2-3 times per month). Key insights:

  • Power users primarily wanted to check their balance and make transfers quickly. They valued speed above all else and were frustrated by "extra taps."
  • Casual users primarily wanted to review transactions and understand their spending. They valued clarity and context — "Where did my money go this month?"
  • Both groups wanted personalization — the ability to pin frequently used features and hide ones they never touch.

Challenge 1

Redesigning the home dashboard

The home screen needed to serve two very different user types: power users who want speed, and casual users who want insight.

Before

Everything-on-one-screen approach. Account balance, 10 recent transactions, 6 quick action buttons, promotional banners, savings progress, and notifications all in a single vertical scroll. No visual hierarchy. Key info buried below the fold.

After

Card-based modular dashboard with clear visual hierarchy. Balance prominent at top with a spending ring. Quick actions (Send, Request, Pay Bills, Top Up) in a fixed row below. Then modular, reorderable cards: Recent Transactions, Spending Insights, Savings Goals. Users can hide/show and drag to reorder cards.

Design Decision: I introduced a "spending ring" — a circular progress visualization showing monthly spend vs. budget — as the hero element below the balance. In user testing, 9/10 participants said this gave them an immediate sense of their financial health "at a glance."

Challenge 2

Streamlining the send-money flow

I consolidated the transfer flow from 6 screens to 3 by combining related inputs and leveraging smart defaults:

New Flow

  1. Screen 1 — Who & How Much: Combined recipient selection and amount entry on a single screen. Recent recipients shown as avatar pills at the top. Keyboard opens automatically. Source account auto-selected (user's primary checking).
  2. Screen 2 — Review: Clean summary showing recipient, amount, source account, estimated arrival. Optional note field (collapsed by default). Edit buttons inline — no back navigation needed.
  3. Screen 3 — Confirmation: Biometric prompt → Success animation → Share receipt option. The entire flow takes under 15 seconds for a repeat transfer.

Micro-interaction: I designed a satisfying "money sent" animation — the amount number shrinks and flies from the source card to the recipient's avatar with a subtle haptic pulse. In testing, users described it as "delightful" and "makes me feel confident the transfer went through."

Challenge 3

Rethinking navigation

I replaced the hamburger menu with a bottom tab bar — the most accessible navigation pattern on mobile. After analyzing feature usage frequency, I selected 5 tabs:

🏠 Home
💳 Cards
📊 Insights
🔔 Activity
👤 Profile

Less frequently used features (Statements, Help, Settings, Referral) were moved under the Profile tab. A global search bar at the top of every screen lets users find any feature by name.

Before

Hamburger menu with 12 items in a flat list. "Find Statements" task: average 34 seconds, 40% failure rate in testing.

After

5-tab bottom navigation + global search. "Find Statements" task: average 8 seconds, 0% failure rate. All primary features reachable in ≤2 taps.

Visual Design

Establishing the visual identity

I developed a fresh visual language that conveys trust and modernity:

  • Color: Primary ocean blue (#0ea5e9) for trust, with teal (#14b8a6) and indigo (#6366f1) accents. Dark mode support with carefully tuned dark surfaces (#0f172a, #1e293b).
  • Typography: SF Pro (iOS) / Roboto (Android) with a clear type scale: 34/28/22/17/15/13. Bold weights for headings, regular for body.
  • Elevation: Subtle card shadows (0 2px 8px rgba(0,0,0,0.06)) rather than borders, creating depth without visual clutter.
  • Iconography: Custom icon set (48 icons) with 2px stroke weight, rounded caps, maintaining visual harmony across the app.
  • Motion: Spring-based animations (damping: 0.8, stiffness: 200) for transitions, giving the app a lively but controlled feel.

Reflection

Lessons learned

  1. Two audiences, one screen: The modular card approach on the dashboard was key to serving both power users and casual users without compromise. Personalization > one-size-fits-all.
  2. Count the taps: In mobile banking, every extra tap erodes trust and increases abandonment. The discipline of consolidating the transfer flow from 6→3 screens had the single biggest impact on user satisfaction.
  3. Sweat the micro-interactions: The "money sent" animation seemed like a nice-to-have, but it became the most-mentioned positive in App Store reviews. Small details compound into emotional quality.
